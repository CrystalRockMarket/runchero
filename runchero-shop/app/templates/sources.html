The pricing rule, in plain text

Our base price for a cut is the latest published benchmark for that cut, less a fixed margin. The default margin is set in the shop admin, and it applies to the base cut price only.

  1. Benchmark. The newest observation for the cut's series, whatever its source.
  2. Base price. Benchmark × (1 − margin), then floored to the nearest cent ending in 9. The default margin is 20%, and that last step can add a few tenths of a point to the discount, never take any away.
  3. Savings badge. Benchmark − base price. It describes the cut before packing options, never after.

Packing, wrapping, and gift options are priced on their own line. They are not folded into the comparison, because a grocery shelf price does not include them either.

How fresh, and how honest

  • Every figure carries its date. The shop shows the day the price was observed, not the day we fetched it.
  • Stale is labeled stale. Anything older than {{ stale_days }} days is marked as such. We would rather show an old number with a warning than quietly quote it as current.
  • Feeds fail quietly. If a source is unreachable, the last good value stays live with its original date. No number is invented or interpolated.
  • Orders snapshot the market. The receipt stores the figures you saw at checkout, so a later refresh cannot rewrite a past order.

What we never do

  • No scraping chain-store websites or delivery apps.
  • No paid price aggregators, and no prices behind a login.
  • No “market price” without a source and a date.
  • No invented local prices. Hand-entered store prices carry the store name, the date, and a note.
  • No blending two sources into one number and calling it USDA.
  • No changing a published figure to make a comparison look better.

What is ours, not the market's

Plenty of what you see here does not come from a federal feed at all. It is ours, and it lives in this shop's own database:

  • Herd, cut sheet, and availability — what we actually have.
  • Orders, packing choices, pickup and delivery windows.
  • Price changes made in the admin console, each with an audit trail and the ability to revert.
  • Hand-entered local store prices, kept separate from the federal figures.

The shop database is a local SQLite file. Order receipts keep their own copy of the market snapshot they were priced against.

Spot something wrong?

If a number looks off, tell us which page and which row. We will re-pull the feed, and if the figure was wrong we will correct it and say so in the price audit log.
